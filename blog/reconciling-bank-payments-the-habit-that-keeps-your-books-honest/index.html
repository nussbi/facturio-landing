Reconciling Bank Payments: The Habit That Keeps Your Books Honest

August 6, 2026 · facturio.ch

Reconciliation means matching every line on your bank statement to an invoice, receipt or transfer in your books. It is the single most effective check on your bookkeeping.

Why reconciliation matters

Reconciliation catches missed payments, double entries and typos before they compound. A bank statement is ground truth — if your books disagree with it, something is wrong.

It flags unpaid invoices quickly. When a payment has not arrived, reconciliation makes the gap obvious instead of letting it hide for months.

It confirms that money actually reached your account. A customer who says they paid is only verified when the payment appears and matches.

Regular reconciliation keeps errors small. The longer you leave it, the harder it is to trace where a discrepancy came from.

  • Catches missed or duplicate entries.
  • Flags unpaid invoices.
  • Confirms money actually arrived.
  • Keeps errors small and traceable.

A simple routine

Once a week, or at least monthly, go through your bank statement and tick off each transaction against your invoices and receipts. Investigate anything that does not match.

Start from the bank statement, since it is the reliable record. Every line should correspond to something in your books.

Work systematically from top to bottom so nothing is skipped. A five-minute weekly pass beats an hour of untangling every quarter.

Record the match as you go, marking each invoice as paid in your system. This keeps your receivables list accurate in real time.

  • Tick off each bank line.
  • Start from the bank statement.
  • Investigate mismatches immediately.
  • Mark invoices as paid as you go.

Handling mismatches

When a payment does not match an invoice, check the amount first. Part-payments and rounding differences are common and usually easy to explain.

Look at the reference number. A missing or wrong reference is the most frequent cause of unmatched payments, especially without structured references.

Contact the customer if the payment is genuinely missing or short. A quick message resolves most discrepancies within days.

Keep a note of unresolved items so they are not forgotten. Left alone, a small mismatch becomes a year-end headache.

  • Check the amount first.
  • Verify the reference number.
  • Contact the customer if needed.
  • Note unresolved items for follow-up.

Automating the matching

Structured references on your invoices let the bank return the reference with the payment. Your software then matches it to the invoice automatically.

A QR reference or creditor reference eliminates manual reading entirely. The number arrives back exactly as it was sent.

Automation turns reconciliation from data entry into review. You confirm the software’s matches instead of finding them yourself.

Use the structured reference on your QR-bills — it lets your bank and accounting software match payments to invoices automatically, turning reconciliation from a chore into a review.
