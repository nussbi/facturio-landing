The Creditor Reference (SCOR / ISO 11649): Structured Matching Without a QR-IBAN

September 14, 2026 · facturio.ch

The creditor reference — also called SCOR or the ISO 11649 reference — is the middle path: structured and automatically matchable, but on your ordinary IBAN.

How it works

The reference starts with "RF" followed by two check digits and a body of up to 21 characters. The check digits let systems validate the reference instantly, catching typos before they cause mismatches.

The format is defined by ISO 11649, an international standard, which is why the same reference works across borders and banking systems.

The check digits are computed from the body using a modulus-97 algorithm, so an incorrectly typed reference is almost always detected on entry.

Because it is self-validating and structured, a creditor reference can be matched to a payment automatically, exactly like the QR reference, but without needing a QR-IBAN.

  • Format: RF + 2 check digits + up to 21 characters.
  • Works with a standard IBAN.
  • Internationally understood (ISO 11649).

Why it beats free text

A free-text reference relies on the customer typing it correctly and your system matching loosely. A structured RF reference is validated by the bank and matched exactly.

Loose matching by name or amount produces false matches when two clients pay the same amount or a name is misspelled. A structured reference removes that ambiguity.

The check digits mean a typo is caught before the payment is sent, rather than surfacing later as an unmatched payment you must chase.

Exact matching means your books reconcile themselves: the payment is applied to the right invoice without you reading a bank statement line by line.

Where it fits on the QR-bill

On a QR-bill, the creditor reference sits in the reference field alongside the standard IBAN. It is one of the supported reference types in the Swiss QR standard.

For most Swiss freelancers and SMEs, the creditor reference is the sweet spot: automated matching with no QR-IBAN and no special bank product.

The QR-bill also supports the 27-digit QR reference, but that requires a QR-IBAN, which is why many businesses choose the creditor reference instead.

A QR-bill can also carry an unstructured message or no reference at all, but that forfeits automatic reconciliation, so the creditor reference is the better default.

Using it in practice

Generate the reference from your invoicing software rather than typing it by hand. A hand-typed RF reference is no better than free text if the check digits are wrong.

Keep one reference per invoice. Reusing the same creditor reference across invoices defeats the purpose, because the system can no longer tell the payments apart.

Send the reference on the invoice and the QR-bill together, so the customer pays with the exact reference your system expects back.
