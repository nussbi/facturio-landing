Invoice Design and Branding: Make It Professional, Not Fancy

September 16, 2026 · facturio.ch

Your invoice is a touchpoint with the client. A clean, branded invoice signals professionalism and reduces the chance of questions that delay payment.

What matters

Consistency matters more than creativity. Use your logo, your standard fonts and colours, and the same layout every time so clients recognise your invoices instantly.

A logo in the header, your company colours used sparingly and a readable font are enough. The invoice should look like it came from the same business as your website and quotes.

Keep the layout stable across all invoices. When the amount, due date and payment details always appear in the same place, the client finds them without hunting.

Whitespace is part of the design. A crowded invoice looks hard to read and is more likely to be set aside, while a spacious one gets processed quickly.

  • Show your logo and brand colours.
  • Keep a consistent layout across all invoices.
  • Make the total and due date impossible to miss.

What hurts

Clutter, tiny fonts and buried payment details slow down payment. Put the amount, the due date and the QR-bill where they are immediately visible.

Too much decoration competes with the numbers. The invoice’s job is to communicate an amount and a way to pay, and anything that distracts from that is working against you.

Inconsistent templates across months make clients unsure whether an invoice is genuine. A client who hesitates is a client who does not pay on time.

Hard-to-read fonts or low-contrast colours cause errors when a client keys the details into their own system. Readability is a payment-speed feature.

The information hierarchy

Order the invoice so the most important facts come first: who is billing whom, how much, and when it is due. The client should grasp all three in a glance.

Put the total in a larger weight near the top or bottom in a fixed position. It is the single number the client acts on, so it must be the easiest thing to find.

Place the payment details and the QR-bill together. The client should never have to search two parts of the page to know how to pay.

Keep supporting detail — line items, VAT, references — clearly structured but visually secondary, so it supports the total without competing with it.

The one thing to get right

The QR-bill is the part the client actually acts on. Make sure it is clear, correct and scannable — a beautiful invoice with a broken QR code is a payment waiting to fail.

Check the QR-bill’s data against the invoice before sending. A mismatch between the printed total and the encoded amount is the most common failure, and it is entirely avoidable.

Print the QR-bill in the standard layout and position. Deviating from the expected placement makes scanning harder even when the data is correct.

  • Lead with total, due date and payment details.
