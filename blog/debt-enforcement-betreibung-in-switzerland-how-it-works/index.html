Debt Enforcement (Betreibung) in Switzerland: How It Works

August 1, 2026 · facturio.ch

Debt enforcement (Betreibung) is the formal Swiss process for collecting unpaid debts. It is powerful, but it should be your last step, not your first.

The process

You file a debt-enforcement request (Betreibungsbegehren) with the debt collection office at the debtor’s place of residence or registered seat.

The debtor receives a payment order (Zahlungsbefehl) setting out the amount claimed and the deadline to respond.

The debtor can then pay, object (Rechtsvorschlag), or ignore the order. Each choice leads to a defined next step in the process.

If the debtor does nothing, you can request continuation of the enforcement, which can lead to seizure of assets or a certificate of loss.

  • File a debt-enforcement request.
  • The debtor receives a payment order.
  • Debtor pays, objects, or ignores it.
  • Continue enforcement if there is no response.

What an objection means

If the debtor objects, the enforcement is suspended. The debt is not cancelled, but you cannot proceed to collection without lifting the objection.

To lift the objection you must prove the debt in court, usually via the fast Rechtsöffnung procedure if you have a signed acknowledgement or judgment.

A signed contract, a recognised invoice or a written acknowledgement can be enough to obtain provisional lifting of the objection.

Without proof, the objection stands and enforcement stalls. This is why documenting the debt from the start is so important.

  • Objection suspends enforcement.
  • Debt is not cancelled, only stalled.
  • Prove the debt to lift the objection.
  • Written proof is decisive.

Costs and timeframes

Debt enforcement is not free. You pay fees to the debt collection office, and court proceedings to lift an objection add legal costs.

These costs can often be added to the claim, but only if the debtor is ultimately found liable. You may have to advance them first.

The process takes time. From filing to a payment order is quick, but a contested objection can drag the matter into months.

Weigh the cost against the amount owed. For very small debts, enforcement may cost more than the debt itself is worth.

  • Office fees apply.
  • Court costs add up if contested.
  • Uncontested steps are quick.
  • Contested objections take months.

Using it strategically

The mere filing of a debt-enforcement request is often enough to prompt payment, because it has real legal consequences. A payment order gets attention that a reminder does not.

Keep it as a tool, not a reflex. Exhaust reminders first, and use enforcement when good faith has clearly run out.

Document every step before you file: the invoice, the reminders, the terms. A clean trail makes both filing and any later proof far easier.
