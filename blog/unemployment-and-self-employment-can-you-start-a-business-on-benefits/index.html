Unemployment and Self-Employment: Can You Start a Business on Benefits?

August 19, 2026 · facturio.ch

If you are on unemployment benefits and want to start a business, Swiss rules allow a transition — but under conditions designed to help you become independent.

The transitional arrangement

You can develop a business while still receiving benefits, provided you remain available to the labour market and the activity is compatible with your job-search obligations. Some cantons offer start-up support.

The unemployment insurance recognises that building a business takes time and allows you to combine planning with your benefit period.

You must still be able and willing to accept suitable employment. The business activity cannot be a reason to turn down a reasonable job offer.

Many regional placement offices (RAV) actively support the transition and can connect you with coaching or advice for new founders.

  • You may develop a business during a transitional period.
  • You must remain available for work.
  • Start-up coaching may be available.

What to avoid

You cannot quietly run a full-time business while claiming full benefits as if you were jobless. Report your activity and your income to the unemployment office.

Any income from the business must be declared and is offset against your benefits. Concealing it is treated as an offence and can lead to repayment demands.

Working full-time in your business while claiming full daily allowances is incompatible with the requirement to remain available for work.

Be honest about the time you spend. Even unpaid business activity that consumes your availability can raise questions.

Be transparent

Tell the RAV (regional placement office) about your plans early. Many offices actively support the transition to self-employment and can advise you on how to do it within the rules.

Early disclosure turns a potential conflict into a supported plan. The office can set expectations and often help with training or advice.

Ask specifically about any start-up support your canton offers. Some run programmes that continue a form of support while you build the business.

Keep the office updated as your plans progress. Surprises — like a sudden jump in business income — are best communicated before they are discovered.

Planning the transition

Write a simple business plan before you approach the office. It shows you are serious and helps the office assess your case.

Think about timing. The business may not generate income for months, so plan how you will cover living costs during the build-up.

Understand how the income you earn will offset your benefits during the transition. This affects how much you can draw while the business grows.

Once the business is genuinely self-sustaining, you can exit the benefit system cleanly and register your self-employment in the normal way.
