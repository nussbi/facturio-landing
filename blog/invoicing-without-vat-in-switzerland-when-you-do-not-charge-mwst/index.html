Invoicing Without VAT in Switzerland: When You Do Not Charge MWST

October 13, 2026 · facturio.ch

Swiss businesses may issue invoices without adding MWST when exempt from tax liability. The right treatment depends on turnover, registration status and what is supplied; “no VAT” alone does not explain why.

When you may invoice without MWST

Most Swiss-based businesses face a CHF 100,000 threshold on worldwide turnover from supplies not exempt without credit. Below it, they are generally exempt from VAT liability unless they register voluntarily.

The test is turnover, not profit, bank balance or Swiss sales alone. Qualifying foreign supplies and the business’s activities are included. Some organisations have special thresholds; check the FTA rules for your case.

Voluntary registration may suit businesses with significant input MWST or VAT-registered clients. It also means charging tax where due, filing returns and retaining evidence. Compare potential deductions with extra administration and pricing effects.

Do not confuse the small-business exemption with exempt, zero-rated or out-of-scope supplies; each has different input-tax and invoice rules. See the FTA overview of liability.

What to show on an invoice with no MWST

Show the parties, issue date, unique invoice number, goods or services, performance date where relevant, agreed total, currency, due date and payment details.

If not VAT-registered, state the amount due without a VAT line or rate. A short note that you are not registered can clarify your status; do not suggest the client can reclaim MWST.

Do not show an MWST number or tax amount as if entitled to charge it. Article 27 of the Federal VAT Act covers tax shown incorrectly; an unauthorised amount can remain payable until corrected.

A QR-bill is a payment instrument, not a VAT decision. Match its amount and reference to the invoice, do not show tax you are not charging, and retain the full invoice.

Bookkeeping and pricing consequences

If not registered, you generally cannot deduct suppliers’ MWST as input tax. Record business-related purchases at gross cost and keep the supporting invoice or receipt.

Agree the price clearly. If you quote CHF 500 without MWST, the invoice total remains CHF 500; do not add tax later without a valid basis and agreement. Business clients cannot reclaim tax not charged.

Keep invoices, credit notes, contracts, supplier documents and payment records together. Reconcile bills with your books and receipts, and track qualifying turnover so you can check the threshold.

A registered business may issue invoices without Swiss MWST for specific exempt or cross-border supplies, but that differs from exemption from registration. Check each supply’s legal treatment before reusing a no-tax template.

When turnover or your status changes

Monitor qualifying turnover and forecast signed or likely contracts. The FTA says liability can arise when the threshold is met or expected to be met; do not wait for year-end.

If liability starts, register with the FTA and update invoicing. Taxable persons must register within 30 days of starting liability. Confirm the effective date and keep the confirmation.

Cross-border work needs separate review: a service may be outside Swiss MWST under place-of-supply rules, while another country requires registration or returns. No Swiss tax on one bill settles neither question.

After registration, update templates with required UID/MWST and tax details. Do not silently edit issued invoices; document corrections and ask the FTA or a fiduciary when timing or treatment is unclear.
