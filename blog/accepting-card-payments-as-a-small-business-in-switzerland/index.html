Accepting Card Payments as a Small Business in Switzerland

July 25, 2026 · facturio.ch

Accepting card payments removes a barrier for your customers and can speed up payment. But it comes with costs worth understanding.

The options

You can accept cards via a payment terminal, an online payment provider, or integrated invoicing tools that add a card-payment option to your invoices. Each has different fees and setup.

A card terminal suits in-person payments in a shop or at a client’s premises. It is fast and familiar for face-to-face transactions.

An online payment provider handles invoices and e-commerce, letting customers pay by card from a link without any terminal.

Integrated card options embed payment directly into your digital invoices, so the customer can pay with one click from the invoice itself.

  • Card terminal for in-person payments.
  • Online payment provider for invoices and e-commerce.
  • Integrated card options on digital invoices.
  • Each has different fees and setup.

The costs

Providers charge a per-transaction fee and sometimes a monthly fee. Typical per-transaction fees are a small percentage of the amount plus a fixed component.

Compare the effective rate against the value of faster payment and fewer unpaid invoices. A 2% fee can be cheap if it eliminates late payments.

Watch for fixed monthly charges, which hurt low-volume businesses disproportionately. A terminal you rarely use may not justify its monthly cost.

Factor in settlement time. Some providers pay out daily, others weekly, and the delay affects your cash flow.

  • Per-transaction fee (percentage plus fixed).
  • Possible monthly fee.
  • Settlement time varies.
  • Compare against the value of faster payment.

Choosing a provider

Compare at least two or three providers on fees, settlement speed and supported payment methods. The cheapest headline rate is not always the best deal.

Check whether the provider supports the cards your customers actually use, and whether it handles recurring or instalment payments if you need them.

Look for Swiss-focused providers that handle local card brands and integrate with Swiss banking and invoicing tools.

Read the contract for minimum terms and cancellation notice. Locking into a bad deal for a year is a common and avoidable mistake.

  • Compare several providers.
  • Check supported payment methods.
  • Prefer Swiss-friendly integration.
  • Watch minimum terms and cancellation.

Matching it to your business

If your customers repeatedly ask to pay by card, offering it will likely reduce late payments. Start with the cheapest option that covers how your customers actually want to pay.

For invoice-based work, an integrated card option is often the lowest-effort route. The customer pays from the invoice and the payment is matched automatically.

For in-person sales, a terminal is the natural fit. For occasional use, consider a provider with low or no monthly fees.
