Digital Bookkeeping in Switzerland: A Practical Guide

August 8, 2026 · facturio.ch

Swiss law fully accepts digital bookkeeping, provided your records are complete, legible and reproducible. Going digital saves time and reduces errors.

What is allowed

You can keep your accounts electronically as long as the data is accurate, can be read at any time, and cannot be altered without detection. Standard accounting software satisfies this.

The key requirements are completeness, legibility and reproducibility. Any format that lets you reprint or reproduce the original document qualifies.

Scanned receipts are legally valid in Switzerland. A legible scan or photo of a receipt carries the same weight as the paper original.

Alterations must be traceable. Use software that logs changes, so an auditor can see what was changed and when.

  • Electronic records are fully accepted.
  • Data must be legible and reproducible.
  • Scans and photos of receipts are valid.
  • Changes must be detectable and logged.

Going paperless in practice

Start by digitising every receipt the moment you receive it. A consistent routine means nothing gets lost between the wallet and the books.

Store everything in one searchable place, ideally software that links documents to the transactions they support. This turns your archive into a working tool.

Keep digital backups in at least two locations. Losing a hard drive should not mean losing a decade of accounting records.

Retain original paper only where the law specifically demands it, which is rare. For almost everything, the digital copy is enough.

  • Digitise receipts immediately.
  • Store in one searchable place.
  • Keep at least two backups.
  • Paper originals are rarely required.

What digital bookkeeping gives you

Automatic calculation removes arithmetic errors and speeds up every entry. Your software adds, categorises and totals for you.

Searchable records mean you can find any invoice, receipt or transaction in seconds. A tax question that once took an afternoon now takes a minute.

Instant reports show your profit, outstanding invoices and cash position on demand. You always know where the business stands.

Digital books are trivial to share with a fiduciary or tax advisor. You can grant read access or export files instead of carting boxes of paper.

  • Fewer arithmetic errors.
  • Searchable and instantly accessible.
  • Real-time reports on demand.
  • Easy to share with your fiduciary.

Choosing the right tools

Choose software that handles Swiss specifics rather than fighting a generic tool. QR-bills, VAT and your canton’s language should all work out of the box.

Look for automated bank reconciliation and payment matching, which save the most time. These features turn bookkeeping from data entry into review.

Ensure the software can export data in a format your fiduciary accepts. Lock-in is a real risk when you change tools or advisors.

Use software that handles Swiss specifics — QR-bills, VAT, and the language of your canton — so you are not fighting the tool at the same time as the bookkeeping.
