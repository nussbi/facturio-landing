Friendly Payment Reminders That Get You Paid Faster

October 3, 2026 · facturio.ch

Late payments are rarely personal. Your client opened your invoice, meant to pay it, and then forgot. A short, friendly reminder is usually all it takes to get the money moving.

When to send the first reminder

Your Zahlungsfrist is the starting point. If your invoice states 30 days, the clock starts on day 31. Reminding someone before the due date looks impatient, but waiting weeks makes you look disorganised.

Send a Zahlungserinnerung three to five days after the due date. This is a gentle, no-consequences nudge, not a formal demand. You simply assume the invoice got lost in a busy inbox.

If you hear nothing after another week, send a second reminder with a firm new deadline. Always reference the original invoice number and amount so the client can find it instantly.

Consistency matters more than perfect wording. A freelancer who always follows up after seven days trains clients to treat their invoices seriously. Automating this schedule means you never miss the window.

What to write (and what not to write)

Keep it to four or five lines. State the invoice number, the amount, the original due date, and ask the client to confirm receipt or make the payment. Anything longer gets skimmed and ignored.

Lead with goodwill. A line like 'in case this invoice slipped through' gives the client a face-saving way to pay without embarrassment. People settle faster when they do not feel accused.

Never threaten in the first or second reminder. Sarcasm, legal jargon, and capital letters turn a simple oversight into a conflict, and a damaged relationship costs more than one late invoice.

Attach a copy of the invoice and include the payment details again. The less the client has to search, the sooner they can click pay. Every extra step is another chance to forget.

Staying friendly while you escalate

After two polite reminders, it is time for a Mahnung. This is a formal reminder that still keeps a professional tone but now states a clear, final deadline for payment.

A Mahnung may also mention Verzugszins. Under Swiss law, default interest is 5 percent per year unless you agreed otherwise, and you can add a reminder fee if it was stated in your terms.

If the Mahnung is ignored, the next step is Betreibung — formal debt collection proceedings through the local debt office. It is the legal escalation, and it rarely destroys a client relationship that was not already lost.

Friendly does not mean patient forever. A clear escalation ladder — reminder, Mahnung, Betreibung — protects your cash flow while keeping your tone professional at every step.
