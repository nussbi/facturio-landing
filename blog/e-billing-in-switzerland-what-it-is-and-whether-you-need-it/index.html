E-Billing in Switzerland: What It Is and Whether You Need It

September 20, 2026 · facturio.ch

E-billing (E-Rechnung) means sending structured, machine-readable invoice data instead of a PDF. It is increasingly relevant as Swiss public bodies move toward it.

Structured vs. PDF

A PDF is a picture of an invoice. It is human-readable, but the recipient’s system cannot extract the data without manual entry or imperfect scanning.

An e-invoice carries the data itself in a structured format, such as a structured XML, so the recipient’s system can read the amount, reference and line items automatically without retyping.

The difference is automation. A structured invoice flows straight into the buyer’s accounting with no human handling, which is why large buyers prefer it.

Hybrid formats like ZUGFeRD embed the structured data inside a PDF, giving you a document that is both human-readable and machine-readable in one file.

  • PDF: human-readable, but needs manual entry.
  • E-invoice: machine-readable, processed automatically.
  • Hybrid formats like ZUGFeRD combine both.

The standards in play

The main structured formats used in Switzerland and Europe are ZUGFeRD, its international counterpart Factur-X, and the XML formats used by specific buyers or sectors.

ZUGFeRD embeds an XML payload in a PDF, making it the easiest step for small businesses because the recipient still sees a normal-looking document.

For public-sector work, the relevant framework is the Swiss e-invoicing initiative, which is moving federal and cantonal bodies toward receiving invoices electronically.

The underlying data model is standardised so that a reference, an amount and a VAT breakdown mean the same thing regardless of which software produced the file.

Who needs it today

Most SMEs do not need e-billing yet, but public-sector suppliers are being transitioned first. If you invoice cantonal or federal bodies, expect an e-billing requirement sooner rather than later.

Large corporate buyers increasingly request e-invoices from their suppliers to cut processing costs. You may be asked to switch before any legal obligation applies to you.

The obligation depends on who you bill, not on your size. A small firm that supplies the public sector can be required to send e-invoices while a larger firm with only private clients is not.

Voluntary adoption still has value. Sending structured invoices reduces manual entry on your client’s side, which can mean faster approval and faster payment.

Preparing without overinvesting

Choose invoicing software that can produce structured data, so you are ready when a client or authority asks for an e-invoice. Adopting early is cheaper than reacting late.

You do not need to rebuild your workflow today. The practical step is to ensure your software keeps clean, structured invoice data rather than only rendering a PDF.

When a client asks for a specific format, confirm the exact standard and version they accept. Sending the wrong flavour of XML is a common and avoidable source of rejection.

Keep your core data clean: correct IBAN, references, VAT and addresses. Structured formats are unforgiving of sloppy data, which is also why they reduce downstream errors.

  • Structured data, not just a rendered PDF, is the future.
  • Public-sector suppliers will be required first.
  • ZUGFeRD is the easiest first step for SMEs.
  • Clean data makes any e-invoice format work.
