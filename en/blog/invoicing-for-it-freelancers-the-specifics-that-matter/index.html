Invoicing for IT Freelancers: The Specifics That Matter

July 2, 2026 · facturio.ch

IT freelancing has its own invoicing patterns: hourly or milestone billing, often for foreign clients, with recurring maintenance work on top.

Your typical invoices

Hourly invoices for development work, milestone invoices for projects, and recurring invoices for maintenance or retainers. Each needs a clear description of the work.

Hourly invoices work best when they are backed by detailed time tracking. The invoice should mirror the time log so the client can see exactly what they paid for.

Milestone invoices tie payment to concrete deliverables rather than hours. Define each milestone clearly in the contract so the invoice is never a surprise.

Recurring invoices cover maintenance, hosting or retainer work. They should state the period and the included scope so the recurring fee is unambiguous.

  • Hourly invoices with detailed time tracking.
  • Milestone invoices tied to deliverables.
  • Recurring invoices for maintenance.

Cross-border reality

Many IT clients are abroad, so cross-border VAT and reverse charge come up constantly. Keep client VAT numbers on file and add the reverse-charge note.

Foreign clients are the norm in IT freelancing, which means you will invoice with reverse charge far more often than a local business would.

Collect and verify each client’s VAT number at the start of the engagement and store it on their record. It becomes a standard field on every invoice.

Add the reverse-charge note as a template default for foreign business clients, so correct VAT treatment happens without a separate decision each time.

  • Keep client VAT numbers on file.
  • Apply reverse charge for foreign B2B.
  • Make the note a template default.

Track time properly

Hourly IT billing lives or dies on good time tracking. Log time as you work, tie it to specific tasks, and let the invoice reflect that detail — it prevents disputes and justifies the bill.

Log time as you work rather than reconstructing it at the end of the month. Reconstructed hours are estimates, and estimates invite challenge.

Tie each time entry to a specific task or ticket. A client who sees exactly what each hour paid for is far less likely to dispute the invoice.

Use the same task descriptions on the invoice as in the time log. Consistency between the two is what makes the bill easy to verify.

  • Log time as you work.
  • Tie entries to specific tasks.
  • Match the invoice to the time log.

Manage scope creep

Scope creep is the constant risk in IT work, and invoicing is where it surfaces. Agree how extra work is billed before it happens.

Define in the contract what is included and how changes are handled. A change request that is priced before it starts rarely becomes a dispute.

Invoice extras promptly and separately, rather than burying them in the next invoice. A separate line keeps the original scope and the extras visible.

When scope expands, confirm the additional cost in writing before doing the work. That single habit prevents most of the disagreement IT freelancers face.
