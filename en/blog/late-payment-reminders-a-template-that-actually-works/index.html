Late Payment Reminders: A Template That Actually Works

August 2, 2026 · facturio.ch

Most late payments are simply forgotten. A well-written reminder recovers the money while preserving the client relationship.

The three-step ladder

Start with a friendly reminder, escalate to a formal dunning notice, and only then consider debt enforcement. Each step adds formality and consequence.

The first step assumes good faith: a short, polite note that the invoice may have been overlooked. Many invoices are paid at this stage.

The second step is formal: restate the amount, set a firm new deadline, and mention the consequences of non-payment, such as dunning fees or interest.

The third step is debt enforcement, which should be your last resort. It has real legal weight and should not be used casually.

  • Friendly reminder (1–2 weeks after due).
  • Formal dunning notice with a new deadline.
  • Debt enforcement as a last resort.
  • Each step adds formality and consequence.

What to include

Reference the invoice number and amount, restate the due date, and give a clear new deadline. Keep the tone factual — the facts do the work, not the emotion.

Include your payment details again, ideally with a fresh QR-bill, so paying is as easy as possible. Friction is the enemy of payment.

State any dunning fee or default interest that now applies, if your terms allow it. This makes the cost of further delay explicit.

Be precise about the action you want: payment of the outstanding amount by the new deadline. A single clear ask is more effective than a vague one.

  • Reference the invoice number and amount.
  • Restate the due date and set a new deadline.
  • Provide payment details and a QR-bill.
  • State any fee or interest now due.

Tone and timing

Send the first reminder soon after the due date, not weeks later. The longer you wait, the more likely the invoice has genuinely been forgotten or deprioritised.

Keep the tone friendly at first. Most late payers are not dishonest, just busy, and a hostile tone damages a relationship over a routine oversight.

Escalate only when a reminder is ignored. Give each step enough time, typically one to two weeks, before moving to the next.

Be consistent. If you send reminders reliably, clients learn that your invoices are always followed up and pay accordingly.

  • Remind soon after the due date.
  • Stay friendly at first.
  • Escalate only when ignored.
  • Be consistent, every time.

Automating the first nudge

A polite reminder sent automatically a few days after the due date recovers a surprising number of payments with zero human effort. Save the personal follow-up for the ones that still do not pay.

Automation removes the awkwardness of chasing. A system-generated reminder feels less personal and less confrontational than a phone call.

Set the automatic reminder to include the invoice and a working payment link or QR-bill, so the customer can settle in one click.
