QR-IBAN Explained: Do You Actually Need One?

September 13, 2026 · facturio.ch

Many freelancers assume they need a QR-IBAN to send QR-bills. You do not. Here is what a QR-IBAN actually is and when it is worth it.

What a QR-IBAN is

A QR-IBAN is a special IBAN issued by your bank or PostFinance that works only together with the 27-digit QR reference. It routes payments through the QR infrastructure so they can be matched automatically.

A QR-IBAN is recognisable by the digits "30" in positions five and six of the IBAN (for example, CHxx 30xx ...). This marks it as a QR-routing account rather than an ordinary payment account.

It cannot be used for ordinary SEPA transfers or manual payments. A QR-IBAN is reserved for QR-bill payments carrying a QR reference.

Because it is a dedicated arrangement, a QR-IBAN is typically a separate account or a routing layer on top of an existing account, set up with your bank.

  • Issued by your bank or PostFinance.
  • Only used with the QR reference.
  • Recognisable by "30" in positions 5 and 6 of the IBAN.

Do you need it?

You need a QR-IBAN only if you want automatic matching via the QR reference. For most SMEs, a standard IBAN with a creditor reference (RF…) gives you the same automation without the special account.

A standard IBAN is fully sufficient for a QR-bill. The QR standard accepts a normal IBAN with a creditor reference or an unstructured message.

The decision is about volume. If you receive many payments and spend hours reconciling them, the QR reference is worth the QR-IBAN; if not, it is an unnecessary complication.

Do not set up a QR-IBAN out of a sense of obligation. It solves a specific problem — high-volume automatic reconciliation — and you should only adopt it when you actually have that problem.

What it costs

A QR-IBAN may come with account fees or require a specific account package, depending on the bank. Ask your bank what the arrangement costs before you open one.

Some banks offer QR-IBANs at little or no extra charge, while others bundle them into business account packages. The cost varies enough that it is worth comparing.

The hidden cost is complexity: a QR-IBAN is one more account detail to keep correct on every invoice, and it is useless for payments outside the QR flow.

Compare the fee against the time you would otherwise spend reconciling. If reconciliation costs you more in hours than the QR-IBAN costs in fees, it pays for itself.

The bottom line

A normal IBAN is fully sufficient for a QR-bill. Choose a QR-IBAN only when your invoice volume justifies the automatic reconciliation it enables.

For EUR invoices, note that since February 2026 a QR-IBAN is not permitted at all — EUR QR-bills use a standard IBAN with a creditor reference.

If you are unsure, start with your existing account and a creditor reference. You can always add a QR-IBAN later when the volume makes it worthwhile.
