The QR Reference (QRR) in Detail: 27 Digits That Automate Reconciliation

September 15, 2026 · facturio.ch

The QR reference (QR-Referenz) is a 27-digit structured number that lets banks match a payment to the exact invoice automatically. It is the most powerful reference type — but it has a requirement.

What it does

The QR reference is embedded in the QR-bill’s payment data. When the customer pays, the reference travels with the payment and your bank or accounting system can reconcile it without any manual work.

Automatic reconciliation is the point: instead of matching payments by name or amount, the system matches by the unique reference, which is reliable even when amounts are identical or names are misspelled.

The reference is built from a 27-digit sequence that includes a check digit, so errors in entry are caught before they cause a mismatch.

For a business with many incoming payments, the QR reference turns hours of manual matching into an automated, near-instant process.

  • 27 digits, including a check digit.
  • Must be used with a QR-IBAN.
  • Enables fully automatic payment matching.

How the check digit works

The 27-digit QR reference uses a modulus-10 check digit, the same scheme used across Swiss payment references for decades. It validates the whole number from a single final digit.

The check digit is computed from the other digits, so a mistyped number almost always fails validation. This is what makes the reference safe to rely on for automatic matching.

The structure is a carry-over from the old red ESR reference (the 26-digit codified line plus a check digit), adapted to 27 digits for the QR standard.

Because the reference is self-validating, a payment cannot silently land on the wrong invoice — a wrong reference is rejected rather than mismatched.

The QR-IBAN requirement

A QR reference only works together with a QR-IBAN, a special IBAN issued by your bank that routes payments through the QR-reference infrastructure. A normal IBAN cannot carry a QR reference.

The QR-IBAN is structurally an IBAN in the CH range, but it is reserved for QR-reference payments and cannot be used for ordinary SEPA or manual transfers.

To use a QR reference you must obtain a QR-IBAN from your bank or PostFinance. It is a separate account arrangement, not something your software can generate.

If you send a QR reference on a standard IBAN, the payment will be rejected or mishandled, because the receiving infrastructure has no way to process it.

Is it worth it?

For high invoice volumes, the QR reference pays for itself in saved reconciliation time. The automation it enables scales with the number of payments you receive.

For low volumes, a creditor reference (RF…) on a standard IBAN is usually the better fit, because it delivers automatic matching without the overhead of a QR-IBAN.

The QR-IBAN may carry a fee or require a specific account product, so weigh that cost against the time you actually spend reconciling payments.

Your bank can tell you the conditions for a QR-IBAN. Ask what it costs before setting one up, and compare it with simply using a creditor reference on the account you already have.

  • QR reference requires a QR-IBAN.
  • It is best for high volumes of incoming payments.
  • For low volumes, an RF creditor reference is usually simpler.
  • Check the bank’s fees before opening a QR-IBAN.
