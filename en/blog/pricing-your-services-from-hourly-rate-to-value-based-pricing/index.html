Pricing Your Services: From Hourly Rate to Value-Based Pricing

July 18, 2026 · facturio.ch

Pricing is one of the highest-leverage decisions in your business. The method you choose changes how much you earn for the same work.

Hourly pricing

Charging by the hour is simple and transparent, but it caps your income at the number of hours you can work and rewards slowness. It is a reasonable starting point.

The client sees exactly what they are paying for, and disputes are rare because time is easy to evidence. That transparency is its main virtue.

The ceiling is real: there are only so many billable hours in a year. Your income is hard-capped by the clock.

It also penalises efficiency. Getting faster at your work means earning less per project, which is the wrong incentive.

  • Simple and transparent.
  • Caps your income at your hours.
  • Rewards time, not results.
  • Penalises efficiency.

Fixed-price packages

Fixed prices charge a set amount for a defined deliverable, decoupling your income from the hours spent. Clients like the certainty of knowing the cost up front.

Packages let you earn more as you get faster. The same deliverable at the same price takes less time, so your effective rate rises.

The risk is scope creep. Define exactly what is included and what costs extra, or a fixed price can turn into unlimited work.

Productising your work into clear packages also makes you easier to buy, which can bring in more clients.

  • Set amount for a defined deliverable.
  • Clients like the certainty.
  • Earn more as you get faster.
  • Define scope to avoid creep.

Value-based pricing

Value-based pricing charges for the outcome, not the hours. If your work saves a client CHF 50,000, a CHF 10,000 fee is a bargain for them and far better for you than hourly billing.

The price is anchored to the value you create, not the effort you spend. This is where pricing becomes a strategic lever rather than a rate card.

It requires understanding the client’s business and being able to articulate the value of the outcome in their terms.

It also requires confidence. You must be willing to name a price based on value and defend it, which takes practice.

  • Charges for the outcome.
  • Uncaps your income.
  • Anchored to value, not effort.
  • Requires confidence and a strong case.

Moving gradually

Start hourly, then shift to fixed-price packages and eventually value-based pricing as you build confidence and proof. Every step up prices your expertise, not just your time.

Raise your rates as you gain proof of results. Evidence of value is what justifies a higher price.

Test higher prices on new clients rather than shocking existing ones. New relationships carry no anchored expectation.
