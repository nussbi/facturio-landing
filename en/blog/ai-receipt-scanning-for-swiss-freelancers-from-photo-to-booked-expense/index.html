AI Receipt Scanning for Swiss Freelancers: From Photo to Booked Expense

October 3, 2026 · facturio.ch

Every Swiss freelancer knows the shoebox: a year of receipts — Migros runs, SBB tickets, the coiffeur in St. Gallen, the parking meter in Bern — stuffed in a drawer until February, when the Steuererklärung looms. AI receipt scanning turns that box into booked expenses in seconds: you snap a photo and the software reads the vendor, date, amount, and VAT rate for you. Here is how it works, why it matters for your MWST, and what to check before you trust it.

How AI receipt scanning works

You photograph a receipt with your phone or drop a PDF into the app. A modern vision model reads the image — not the brittle OCR of a few years ago, which misread 7s as 1s and gave up on a folded corner.

The model extracts the fields you actually need: the vendor, the date, the gross amount, the currency, and — where visible — the VAT rate on the receipt.

For Swiss receipts the AI is trained to recognise the usual rates. A restaurant bill at 8.1 percent, a food item at 2.6 percent, a hotel stay at 3.8 percent, or a receipt marked 0 percent.

Foreign-currency receipts are converted to CHF at the daily rate, so an invoice from a supplier in Germany lands in your books already in Swiss francs.

Why it matters for Swiss freelancers

Time is the obvious win. Typing receipts by hand takes minutes each; a scan takes seconds. Over a year, that is hours you get back — hours better spent on paid work.

The VAT side matters more. When every receipt carries its correct MWST rate, your Vorsteuer (input tax) is captured line by line, so the quarterly VAT return is already half done.

It also removes the lost-receipt problem. A photo captured at the moment of purchase lives in the app and in the cloud, not in a jacket pocket.

And because the data is structured — vendor, date, amount, category — you can finally see where your money goes, instead of guessing at year end.

What to check before you trust it

AI scanning is fast, but it is not a bookkeeper. Check the extracted amount against the receipt before you accept it, especially for hand-written or faded thermal receipts.

Verify the VAT rate. A receipt may show several rates, or none at all; the software may guess, and a wrong rate quietly distorts your VAT return.

Illegible scrawls are the honest failure case — good software will ask you rather than invent a number. That is still faster than typing the whole thing yourself.

Keep the original. Swiss law requires you to retain receipts for ten years (Art. 958f OR); a scan is convenient, but the paper or PDF must remain retrievable.

Fitting it into your bookkeeping

Expense scanning is most useful when it feeds the rest of your books. The captured expenses should flow straight into your accounting, categorised and VAT-tagged.

Pair it with bank-statement import so payments are matched to invoices and expenses are matched to the account, and your reconciliation is largely automatic.

At quarter end, the MWST numbers are already in place — you copy them across rather than reconstructing them from a pile of paper.

And it pairs naturally with invoicing: the same software that books your expenses should generate the SIX-compliant QR-bill for your clients, so income and expenses live in one place.
